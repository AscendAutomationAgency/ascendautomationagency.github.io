# Upwork Automation Market: The Year in Review
## May 2025 – May 2026 (13 Months)

**Report Date:** June 2026
**Data:** 57,306 unique Upwork job postings mentioning Make.com, n8n, or Zapier
**Method:** All figures recomputed from scratch from the 13 raw monthly exports. Where this report contradicts a prior monthly post, the prior post was wrong; see the companion [Corrections & Methodology Appendix](year-in-review-corrections-appendix.md).

---

## Why This Report Exists

For twelve months we published a monthly "State of the Upwork Automation Market" post. Each one read the latest month's numbers and told a story about what they meant. Now that we have a full year (plus a 13th month, May 2026, that no monthly post has seen yet), we went back, recomputed every number from the raw data, and asked an uncomfortable question: **were we right?**

The honest answer: **the recurring monthly narrative was mostly noise dressed up as structure, and several specific claims were simply wrong.** A few calls held up well. This report separates the two, corrects the record, and gives you the insights that actually survive a full year of data.

If you acted on our month-to-month advice, this is the report that tells you which of it to keep and which to discard.

---

## The Single Most Important Correction: The Baseline Was Broken

Every monthly post measured growth "since May 2025." **May 2025 is missing its first five days** — the data begins May 6 (a known, footnoted deletion). May therefore contains ~4,256 jobs across 26 days (~164/day). A complete May would have been roughly **5,050 jobs — essentially identical to June's 5,143.**

This one fact poisons a huge share of what we reported:

- The "explosive summer growth" (May 4,256 → July 5,522, "+30%") was mostly an artifact. On a complete-month basis the real May→July change was closer to **+9%**. The market didn't boom in summer; May was just undercounted.
- **Every "+X% since May" statistic in every post is inflated**, because the denominator was too small.
- Our most-repeated platform claim — *"n8n is uniquely resilient"* — is largely a product of this same flaw (see below).

We knew the May data was incomplete (we footnoted it every month) but we never corrected the math for it. That was a mistake, and it compounded for a year.

**For this report, where a clean baseline matters, we use June 2025 — the first complete month.**

## The Corrected Big Picture

| Metric | May 2025\* | Peak | May 2026 | Honest read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly jobs | 4,256\* | 5,522 (Jul) | 3,519 | Gradual decline; −31.6% from June, −36% from July peak |
| Avg hourly rate | $39.10 | $43.27 (Feb) | $40.63 | Range-bound $38–41 all year; Feb was the lone outlier |
| **Median hourly rate** | **$35** | **$35** | **$35** | **Flat at $35 in 12 of 13 months** |
| Jobs ≥ $75/hr | 153 | 214 (Jul/Aug) | 138 | Premium *count* fell with volume |
| AI-mention share | 44.6% | 58.7% (May'26) | 58.7% | Genuinely accelerating, especially in 2026 |
| Fixed-price share | 29.6% | 37.4% (May'26) | 37.4% | Slow, steady shift to fixed-price work |

\*May 2025 is missing its first five days; treat it as a floor, not a true month.

**The market that actually existed this year:** a gradually contracting one (from ~5,100/month in summer to ~3,500/month by May 2026), where the *typical* job paid a flat $35/hr the entire time, the *average* drifted in a narrow $38–41 band, AI went from "half of jobs" to "nearly 60% of jobs," and clients slowly shifted toward fixed-price contracts. That's the whole story. It is far less dramatic than twelve months of posts made it sound.

## What We Got Wrong

### 1. We called a "structural shift above $40/hr." There wasn't one.
The January and February posts declared rates had structurally re-based above $40 and that "commodity work has been permanently eliminated." The reality across 13 months:

`$39.10 → 39.35 → 39.61 → 39.07 → 38.74 → 38.17 → 38.79 → 37.58 → 40.20 → 43.27 → 40.66 → 39.46 → 40.63`

This is a **range-bound series, not a step-change.** February's $43.27 was a single-month outlier — almost certainly Q1 enterprise budget timing — and it reverted immediately. The median rate, meanwhile, never moved off **$35/hr**. The "structural shift" was the mean being temporarily levered up by a handful of high-rate postings.

### 2. We said "commodity work has been permanently eliminated." It's still the majority of the market.
Through every month, roughly **55–60% of hourly jobs posted at ≤ $40/hr**, and the median held at $35. The low end never disappeared — we just stopped counting it as "the market" once it became convenient to talk about a "specialist-only economy." That framing was wrong and, for a junior consultant reading our posts, actively misleading.

### 3. "n8n is uniquely resilient / technical depth wins in a downturn." Mostly a baseline artifact.
This was our most-repeated platform thesis. Measured the way we measured it (since the broken May baseline), n8n looks resilient (−1.9%) while Zapier (−20.7%) and Make (−22.5%) cratered. **Measured from the first complete month (June), all three are the same:**

| Platform | Since May 2025\* | **Since June 2025** |
|---|---|---|
| Zapier | −20.7% | **−30.5%** |
| n8n | −1.9% | **−27.9%** |
| Make.com | −22.5% | **−30.7%** |

n8n only "won" because it had the largest May→June jump (+36%) — precisely the month the missing week distorts most. On honest footing, **there was no uniquely resilient platform. All three declined ~30%.** The platform horse-race we narrated every month was noise.

### 4. The monthly platform "leadership" drama was almost entirely noise.
- **Nov 2025:** "n8n overtakes Zapier as market leader — historic shift, structural, not temporary." It reversed the *very next month.* Zapier led in 12 of 13 months; n8n led exactly once.
- **Mar 2026:** "n8n's decline is broken (+4.3%)." **Apr 2026:** "n8n's recovery thesis has failed (−14.5%)." **May 2026:** n8n +3.4% again. It was bouncing in a 1,250–1,470 band the whole time. We called three "turning points" in three months on what was statistical wobble.

### 5. We called the "floor" repeatedly and were wrong each time.
- October: "the market has found its floor around 5,000." → November fell to 4,203.
- January: "market floor confirmed at 3,945, contraction over." → February fell to 3,690.
- March: "equilibrium at 3,900–4,000." → April fell to 3,645, May to 3,519.

The market never stopped drifting down. We kept mistaking a single up-month for the bottom.

### 6. "The premium tier is expanding." The premium *count* shrank.
Jobs ≥ $75/hr: 153 (May) → 214 (summer) → **138 (May 2026).** The premium *share* rose only because total volume fell faster than premium volume. We repeatedly framed a shrinking denominator as a "premium boom." The number of high-paying jobs available actually *declined* over the year.

### 7. Specific factual errors (full list in the appendix):
- **September 2025** reported Make.com at **1,857 jobs (−11.6%)**. Actual: **967 (−17.6%)** — a hard error, off by ~90%.
- **March 2026** called generic-CRM mentions of 1,434 an "all-time high." July (1,507) and August (1,511) were both higher.
- **August 2025**'s headline "5,990 platform jobs" counted *platform mentions*; from September on, the headline number silently switched to *unique jobs*. Readers comparing the two saw a phantom crash.

## What We Got Right

### 1. CRM specialization — especially GoHighLevel — was the correct call, all year.
This is the one thesis the full year vindicates. GoHighLevel grew from 561 (May) to **763 (May 2026), +36%** — the strongest sustained growth of any tracked application, and it *held near its highs even in the worst contraction months.* HubSpot was rock-steady (334 → 385). The advice to pair platform skills with a CRM specialty was right, and GoHighLevel-for-agencies was the single best-supported recommendation we made.

One caveat we over-sold: the month-to-month CRM "rotation" (Salesforce up, then Zoho up, then GoHighLevel up...) was mostly small-number noise. Zoho in particular swung wildly (82 to 175 and back) and ended *down* over the year. "Pick a CRM and go deep" was good advice; "Salesforce is surging, pivot to it this month" was noise-chasing.

### 2. "AI is becoming table stakes" was directionally right — and accelerating.
AI-mention share sat around 44–47% through 2025, then climbed: 51% (Mar), 52% (Apr), **59% (May 2026).** By the end of the window, nearly six in ten automation jobs referenced AI in some form. The specific niche calls (AI voice agents in March, "Claude engineering" in April) were anecdotal — based on a handful of postings, not systematic tracking — but the underlying trend is real and is the clearest emerging signal in the dataset.

### 3. "Make.com is the weakest of the three platforms" held up.
Make ended the year as the smallest platform with the deepest decline from its peak. Treating it as a secondary skill rather than a primary specialization was sound.

## A New Insight We Missed All Year: The Shift to Fixed-Price Work

We never tracked it monthly, but it's one of the clearest trends in the data: the **fixed-price share of jobs rose steadily from 29.6% (May 2025) to 37.4% (May 2026)** — a ~26% relative increase, monotonic across the year. Combined with the flat $35 median hourly rate, this suggests clients are increasingly scoping automation as defined deliverables rather than open-ended hourly engagements. For consultants, that argues for **outcome/package pricing** far more concretely than any of our "raise your rates" exhortations did.

## Out-of-Sample Test: Did April's Predictions Hold?

April 2026 made specific Q2 forecasts. May 2026 (which no prior post has seen) lets us grade them honestly:

| April prediction | May 2026 actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Volume ~3,500–3,900 | 3,519 | ✅ Hit (low end) |
| Rates revert to $38–40, "$40 floor was not structural" | $40.63 | ❌ Miss — popped back above $40 |
| "n8n recovery thesis has failed / broad-market story broken" | n8n +3.4% to 1,294 | ❌ Overstated — it bounced |
| CRM resilience continues | GoHighLevel 763 (new high), HubSpot 385 | ✅ Hit |
| AI/Claude penetration rising | AI share 51.8% → 58.7% | ✅ Hit |

April got volume, CRM, and AI right — and over-corrected into pessimism on rates and n8n, **exactly mirroring the optimistic over-correction of January/February.** This is the year in microcosm: every month over-weighted the most recent print.

## The Honest Through-Line

The defining error of the year was **structural over-interpretation of single-month noise.** "Floor confirmed." "Historic shift." "Decline broken." "Recovery failed." "Structural shift above $40." Nearly every one of these was a one-month wobble that reversed within one to two months. The data was telling a calm story — slow volume decline, flat median pay, rising AI, steady CRM demand — and we kept narrating a thriller on top of it.

The lesson for next year's reporting: **trend lines, not turning points.** A single month is noise. Three months is a hint. Six months is a trend.

## What the Full Year Actually Tells a Consultant

1. **Volume is structurally lower and still drifting down.** Plan for a ~3,300–3,700 job/month market, not the 5,000+ of summer 2025. There will be more competition per posting. Proposal volume and differentiation both matter more.
2. **The typical job pays $35/hr and has all year.** If you're a generalist, that's your gravity. Escaping it requires a *real* specialization, not a repositioning statement.
3. **CRM — especially GoHighLevel — is the most durable specialization in this dataset.** Twelve months of data point the same direction. If you take one bet, take this one.
4. **AI fluency is no longer optional.** Nearly 60% of jobs now reference it. This isn't a premium niche anymore; it's the baseline expectation. The premium is in *building* (production agents, integrations), not *mentioning* it.
5. **Clients increasingly want fixed-price deliverables.** Package your work. The hourly-rate hand-wringing matters less than being able to quote a defined outcome.
6. **Ignore the platform horse-race.** Zapier is the volume leader, n8n the technical-depth option, Make the weakest — and all three declined ~30% from peak. Pick based on the clients you want, not on last month's growth arrow.
7. **Premium work is real but scarce and getting scarcer** (138 jobs ≥$75/hr in May 2026, the lowest in the dataset). It exists; there just isn't a lot of it. Win it with proof, not positioning language.

## Methodology & Honesty Notes

- All 13 months recomputed from raw CSVs with a single consistent keyword/rate method. Where prior posts used different definitions, those are documented in the [Corrections Appendix](year-in-review-corrections-appendix.md).
- **Keyword-mention counting** overcounts: one job mentioning two platforms is counted under both. "Platform jobs" totals in some early posts summed these mentions; this report uses **unique job counts** throughout.
- **Rates** are posted hourly maximums where present; ~42–48% of jobs post an hourly rate. Fixed-price budgets are tracked separately and not blended into the hourly average.
- **AI-mention** detection uses word-boundary matching on a fixed term set; it is indicative of a trend, not a precise census, and the term set differs slightly from what some 2025 posts used.
- **May 2025 is incomplete** (missing May 1–5). It is shown for continuity but should not be used as a growth baseline; June 2025 is the first complete month.
