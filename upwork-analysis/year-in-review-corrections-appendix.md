# Corrections & Methodology Appendix
## Companion to the May 2025 – May 2026 Year in Review

This appendix documents every material error, methodology inconsistency, and overstated claim found when all 13 months were recomputed from the raw CSVs. It exists so the record is honest and so readers can see exactly where prior monthly posts diverged from the data.

Severity key: 🔴 hard factual error · 🟠 methodology inconsistency · 🟡 overstated interpretation

---

## 🔴 Hard Factual Errors

### C-1. September 2025: Make.com job count off by ~90%
- **Claimed (Sep post):** "Make.com: 1,857 jobs (−11.6%) — second consecutive double-digit decline."
- **Actual:** **967 jobs (−17.6%** vs August's 1,174).
- **Notes:** The claimed figure (1,857) was *higher* than August's actual (1,174), which contradicts the post's own "decline" language. Confirmed wrong three ways: the October post's "+6.2% to 1,027" implies September ≈ 967; every later 12-month table shows 967; the raw September CSV yields 967. Downstream "all platforms still have 1,800+ jobs each" was therefore also false.

### C-2. March 2026: "Generic CRM mentions hit an all-time high (1,434)"
- **Claimed:** March's 1,434–1,436 generic-CRM mentions were an all-time high.
- **Actual:** March = 1,436, but **July 2025 (1,507) and August 2025 (1,511) were both higher.** March was the highest *since summer*, not all-time. Repeated in the March post, April dashboard, and April key-takeaways.

### C-3. May 2025: Different methodology produced non-comparable headline numbers
- **Claimed (May post):** GoHighLevel "983 mentions"; average hourly rate "$27.79/hr."
- **Recomputed (consistent method):** GoHighLevel **561**; average hourly rate **$39.10/hr.**
- **Notes:** The first post used a different keyword set (it also tracked Pabbly, Twilio, Stripe, WordPress, Webflow, etc.) and a different rate calculation (it appears to have averaged across displayed rate *ranges* rather than the posted hourly maximum). None of the May post's headline figures are directly comparable to later months.

## 🟠 Methodology Inconsistencies

### C-4. The "since May" baseline is structurally invalid
- **May 2025 is missing its first five days** (data starts May 6). May contains 4,256 jobs over 26 dated days (~164/day); a complete month projects to **~5,050**, essentially equal to June's 5,143.
- **Impact:** Every "+X% since May" statistic across all 12 posts is inflated. The "summer growth boom" (May 4,256 → July 5,522) is largely an artifact; the complete-month change is ~+9%, not ~+30%. The deletion was footnoted monthly but never corrected for in any growth calculation.
- **Fix used in the review:** June 2025 (first complete month) is the clean baseline.

### C-5. "Platform jobs" headline changed definition mid-series
- **August 2025 post:** headline "5,990 platform jobs" = the **sum of platform mentions** (Zapier 2,663 + n8n 2,155 + Make 1,174 = 5,992).
- **September 2025 onward:** the headline number became **unique jobs** (Sep 5,072).
- **Impact:** A reader comparing August's "5,990" to September's "5,072" sees a phantom −15% crash; the real unique-job change was August 5,402 → September 5,072 = **−6.1%**. Mention-summing also double-counts every job that names more than one platform.

### C-6. Model and method changed almost every month
The monthly posts were generated by a rotating set of models (Opus 4 → Opus 4.1 → Sonnet 4.5 → Opus 4.5 → Opus 4.6 → Opus 4.7) with no frozen analysis pipeline. Keyword lists, rate handling, and the definition of "premium" drifted across months. This is the root cause of several inconsistencies above. The year-in-review uses one script and one method for all 13 months.

### C-7. AI-mention share is not a like-for-like series
- **September post:** "39.4% of jobs mention AI."
- **Recomputed (consistent word-boundary term set):** September = 43.7%.
- Not a hard error — the term sets differ — but the monthly AI percentages are not directly comparable to each other. The *direction* (rising, accelerating in 2026 to 58.7% by May) is robust; the absolute monthly values are method-dependent.

## 🟡 Overstated Interpretations (claims the full year contradicts)

### C-8. "Structural shift above $40/hr" (Jan & Feb posts)
The 13-month average-rate series is range-bound ($37.58–$43.27), not a step-change. February's $43.27 was a single-month outlier and reverted within one month. **The median rate was $35/hr in 12 of 13 months** (December: $30). No structural re-basing occurred.

### C-9. "Commodity work has been permanently eliminated" (Sep/Nov/Dec/Jan/Feb)
Roughly **55–60% of hourly jobs posted at ≤ $40/hr in every month**, and the median never left $35. The low end of the market persisted throughout. This claim was false and, for newer consultants, misleading.

### C-10. "n8n is uniquely resilient / technical depth wins" (Sep–Mar, recurring)
An artifact of the broken May baseline. On a June baseline, all three platforms declined ~30% (Zapier −30.5%, n8n −27.9%, Make −30.7%). n8n's apparent resilience came entirely from its outsized May→June jump (+36%), the month most distorted by the missing week.

### C-11. "Historic shift: n8n overtakes Zapier as market leader… structural, not temporary" (Nov)
True for one month only (Nov: n8n 1,729 vs Zapier 1,662). Zapier led in **12 of 13 months**; the "shift" reversed in December. Labeling a one-month, 67-job gap "structural" was unsupported.

### C-12. Three n8n "turning points" in three months (Mar–May)
- March: "n8n's decline is broken (+4.3%)."
- April: "n8n's recovery thesis has clearly failed (−14.5%); first negative total growth."
- May 2026 (out-of-sample): n8n **+3.4% to 1,294**, total back to −1.9% since May.
n8n oscillated in a 1,250–1,470 band; each "turning point" was noise.

### C-13. Repeated "the market has found its floor"
- October: floor "around 5,000." → November 4,203.
- January: floor "confirmed at 3,945." → February 3,690.
- March: "equilibrium 3,900–4,000." → April 3,645, May 3,519.
The market declined every quarter; no called floor held.

### C-14. "The premium tier is expanding"
Jobs ≥ $75/hr fell 153 → 138 over the window (peak 214 in summer). Only the *share* rose, because total volume fell faster. A shrinking denominator was repeatedly presented as premium growth.

### C-15. "Salesforce is the only CRM growing" (Sep)
Salesforce was +71.3% since May at that point (87 → 149, arithmetically correct), but GoHighLevel (561 → 662) and others were also up since May. The exclusivity claim was false.

## Claims That Checked Out

For balance, these prior figures were verified accurate against the raw data:
- Monthly unique-job totals from September 2025 onward (the −6.1%, −16.5%, −6.3%, +0.2%, −6.5%, +6.7%, −7.5% MoM series).
- Ultra-premium ($150+) counts cited in 2026 posts (Jan ~21, Feb 29, Mar 15, Apr 19 — all match).
- GoHighLevel's March "all-time high 693" (correct at the time; later exceeded by May 2026's 763).
- The accounting-automation growth callouts (QuickBooks/Xero since-May figures matched, subject to the C-4 baseline caveat).
- The core CRM-specialization and "AI becoming table stakes" theses (directionally validated by the full year).
